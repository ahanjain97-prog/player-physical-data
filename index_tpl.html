How much weight each metric carries

Two separate questions decide that. Repeatability is how well a metric holds up for the same player across consecutive seasons, measured within his own position group (367 repeat players). Team share is how much of the spread inside a pool is explained simply by which club he plays for, corrected for chance by permutation. A metric can repeat well and still tell you more about the team than the player — high acceleration count does exactly that.

The short version. Sprint count and sprint distance are the closest thing here to a measure of the athlete: they repeat about as well as anything in the file and are the least explained by his club. Distance per 90 and meters per minute are reliable numbers but heavily role- and system-driven — they describe the job he is asked to do more than what he is capable of. Acceleration and deceleration counts are the most club-explained figures on the card. Max speed is the one to distrust: repeatability of 0.25, and the median rose about 1.2 km/h in 2026 in all three leagues at once, which is a measurement change rather than players getting faster. Two coverage notes: MLS NEXT Pro 2024 holds tracking for only 26% of that season, and raw values are never comparable across seasons or leagues — use the percentiles.
